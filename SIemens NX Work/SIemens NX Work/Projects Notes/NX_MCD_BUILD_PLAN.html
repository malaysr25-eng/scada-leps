Written 2026-09-18, for the build weekend before the lab visit on Monday 2026-09-21. You build in NX on the college PC; this page carries the numbers, the order of work and the resources. The MATLAB model stays the physics of record — NX MCD adds 3-D motion, collision and a credible visual, and it is where you learn virtual-commissioning tooling.

0 · Before you touch NX

Fifteen minutes, at the lab PC.

What a text scan of that file says it holds: about 2 rigid bodies, 2 collision bodies, 4 sliding joints, 4 signal adapters and 17 signals, plus an OPC UA client pointed at opc.tcp://172.21.30.157 — the cell's own PLC address. The signal names already include Proximity_sensor1..10, Conveyor1..3, Solenoid_valve1..12, Engraving_Completed, Current_Position_Fb and MODE_HIGH_SPEED.

So the signal side is worth keeping — those names match the real PLC tags, and MCD maps signals to an OPC server automatically when names match. The mechanics are one station at best. The cell has 11 actuators, 3 conveyors and 10 sensors, so most of the building is ahead of you.

1 · What you are building

A block leaves a magazine, rides belt 1, is pushed onto belt 2, stops at prox 7, and cylinder A pushes it into the engraver. The platform lifts it, the operator engraves and presses ENG DONE, the platform lowers, cylinder B pushes it to belt 3, belt 3 carries it to prox 9, and outfeed A ejects it into the bin. A second product skips engraving: at prox 8, SV11 pushes it straight across to belt 3.

In MCD that is one rigid body (the block), three transport surfaces (the belts), eleven sliding joints (the rods) plus one for the engraving platform, ten collision sensors (the proximity sensors), and signals named after the PLC's tags.

2 · The order of work

MCD punishes building in the wrong order: a joint on a body that is not rigid does nothing, and a sensor with no collision body never trips.

5 · The weekend, session by session

Each session is self-contained, so stopping early still leaves something that runs.

Friday 1–2 hours · get MCD moving
  1. The checklist in §0.
  2. Block → rigid body; block and belt tops → collision bodies. Play, and watch it sit on the belt.
  3. Transport surface on belt 1 at 210 mm/s. Play. The block rides.
  4. Stop when that works. That is the whole of basic physics; everything after it is repetition.
Saturday 3–4 hours · station 1 end to end
  1. Sliding joints for the five magazine pushers, limits 0–160 mm.
  2. Position control: speed 148 mm/s, target 82 mm.
  3. Collision sensors for prox 1–6.
  4. Signals: Solenoid_valve1..6, Conveyor1, Proximity_sensor1..6.
  5. Signal adapters: each sensor → its signal; Solenoid_valve1 → pusher 1's target; Conveyor1 → belt 1's speed.
  6. Check: pusher out 10.24 s, belt 1 on 13.29, prox 6 at 16.44.
Sunday 3–4 hours · stations 2 and 3
  1. Transport surfaces for belts 2 and 3, 310 and 150 mm/s.
  2. Joints and position control for SV6, cylinder A, cylinder B, SV11, SV12, SV13.
  3. The engraving platform: a sliding joint in +z, 50 mm, rising in 7.63 s (≈ 6.6 mm/s).
  4. Collision sensors for prox 7–10.
  5. The rest of the signals and adapters.
  6. Sequence Editor: build the cycle from §4 and run it with no PLC. That is your demonstration.

Monday at the lab: the measurements on the lab sheet, and job 5 decides the live route — is OPC UA switched on at the CPU? If it is, MCD's existing OPC UA client can point at the real PLC and the model follows the machine.

6 · Connecting it to something, later

  1. The Sequence Editor. MCD drives itself. No PLC, no extra licence, works today. Use this for the report and the viva.
  2. A replay server. A small OPC UA server that publishes the cell's tag names and plays back a validated MATLAB cycle. In NX you point the existing OPC UA client at it instead of the PLC, and MCD maps the signals automatically because the names already match. 3-D motion driven by the calibrated physics, with no machine involved.
  3. The real PLC over OPC UA, the same client pointed at opc.tcp://172.21.30.157, once OPC UA is enabled on the CPU.

Worth knowing before you start: MCD can map external signals to MCD signals automatically when the names match. So give every signal the PLC's own tag name — Solenoid_valve7, not CylA_out.

8 · What to write down as you go

  • Which NX version and which MCD licence the lab PC has.
  • For every MCD object you create: the number you typed, and where it came from — this page, or your own measurement.
  • Anything MCD would not let you do, and the message it gave.
  • A screen recording of the finished cycle. It is the deliverable a viva actually sees.

The rule that has served this project: if you had to guess a number, mark it as a guess in your notes. Don't let it become a fact by being typed twice.
